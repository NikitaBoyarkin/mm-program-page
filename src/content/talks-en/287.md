Using a hypothetical case of spam in comments on public posts, we will look at how an analyst can identify suspicious user behavior. We will talk about how to look for behavioral and technical signals, formulate rules for detecting spam, test them on historical data and evaluate the quality of triggers. We will separately discuss the trade-off between missed violations and false positives, and also why one rule is not enough and how violators' behavior can change in response to restrictions.

The case will be based on synthetic data and illustrate the analytical process rather than building an antifraud system from scratch.
