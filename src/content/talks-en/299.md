Managerial decision-making amid frequent shifts in priorities requires rethinking the basic operational routine. The talk examines an engineering approach to personal management: creating an AI operating system based on autonomous agents. This is not a set of disparate prompts, but an architecture consisting of 17 roles with clear competence boundaries and 13 regular procedures embedded in the real daily work of the director of an analytical function.

The speaker will break down the mechanics of the system in detail. You will learn how a radical reduction in the cost of management procedures changes the decision-making process: for example, a preliminary breakdown of a task that previously required two hours and was often ignored now takes 30 minutes and is always performed algorithmically. The audience will study the structure of "rehearsal panels" — AI profiles of real stakeholders that allow running an argument through criticism before a meeting. A separate block is devoted to an automatic context layer that deterministically loads goals, current focus and open threads into every session. The speaker will also break down system failures, including an incident where four independent agents generated a convincing but completely false conclusion.

Attendees will get a scheme for assessing the cost of their own management procedures in order to transfer them into the AI loop. The talk will give concrete mechanics for assembling the context layer and setting up isolated AI critics for preparing for reviews, difficult 1-1s or budget defenses. As a result you will learn how to turn work with LLMs from chaotic requests into a sustainable management method whose architecture has proven its viability both in an enterprise environment and in the production of a medtech startup.
