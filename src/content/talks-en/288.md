AI agents are already helping to perform entire work tasks. This became possible thanks to the development of both the models themselves and the environment they work in — tools, skills, and feedback mechanisms. But how far have we progressed over the past year? And what capabilities are available today without a subscription to Claude Code, Codex and other cutting-edge solutions?

In this talk we will trace the evolution of agents and break down how improvements in different parts of the system add up to a practical result. We will discuss the capabilities of small models and the role of the harness — the environment that organizes the agent's work. We will assess which general-purpose tasks are solvable right now on a fully open stack. And with our own experiments with Goose, Qwen and DeepSeek we will show which scenarios already work and where limitations remain.

Key questions covered in the talk:

* what has changed in AI agent capabilities over the past year;

* how close modern open models have come to the flagships (current and last year's);

* why the harness and skills can matter more than the model itself;

* when a self-directed agent is useful and when a strict pipeline is needed;

* how to evaluate the practical value of AI progress;

* which tasks are solvable on the open stack right now;
