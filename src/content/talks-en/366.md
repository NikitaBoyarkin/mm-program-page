Customer service is traditionally optimized by operational metrics: cost per contact, response speed (SLA), and the automation percentage. However, this approach ignores the real long-term economic value of service to the business. At T-Bank, they decided to test the hypothesis that support quality is not an abstract good but a manageable economic parameter that can and should be deliberately changed depending on the segment. The talk covers a large-scale experiment on abandoning a single service standard and moving to distributing quality based on expected return on investment (LTV).

The speaker will reveal the mechanics of differentiating service into three modes: strict ECO with a 30% cost reduction (more persistent bots, increased SLAs, and a focus on speed for unprofitable customers and “troublemakers”), standard BASE, and expensive CX+, where the cost of service grows by 70% due to priority, dedicated experts, and motivation for full problem resolution for newcomers and the upper mass segment. You will learn how to build a system of proxy metrics to assess the impact of such modes on long-term LTV, transactional activity, and retention.

The test results proved that investment in CX+ goes far beyond service indicators: segment-weighted NPV grew by 2.2%, activations of new products jumped (for example, MVNO issuances by 3.3% and credit cards by 2.2%), and even the probability of default decreased — customers with quality service statistically significantly less often go delinquent (the Roll Rate 0 share grew by 0.24 pp). Listeners will get an analytical framework for moving to the incremental value of service: how to safely save where it does not destroy LTV, and pour budgets into support where it brings multiple returns.
