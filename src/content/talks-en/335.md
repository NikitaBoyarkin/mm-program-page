Building a mobile tracker capable of competing on equal terms with the global leaders of the attribution market requires an uncompromising architecture. The system must process tens of thousands of events per second, guarantee data consistency, and at the same time remain economically viable. The talk examines the engineering underside of the production realtime attribution system TraceHub, which was designed from scratch and brought to a load of 26,000 events per second. This is not a theoretical overview of technologies, but an honest breakdown of one production pipeline with concrete cost figures per million events at every stage.

The speaker will present the results of a direct benchmark of databases for the hot state on the same code (Cassandra vs ScyllaDB vs YugabyteDB vs YDB), clearly showing why transactionality can make a database 20 times slower for a specific task. You will learn how the three-tier storage architecture is built (Scylla for the hot state, ClickHouse exclusively for aggregates and Iceberg for eternal storage of raw logs), and what real price the business pays for quorum when reading from neighboring availability zones.

Separate attention will be paid to non-obvious optimization lessons. Kirill will break down why rewriting in Rust does not help if the business logic runs into waiting for the network, how the default 5 ms pause in Kafka ate up 28% of processing time, and how to properly build a ladder of retries and a DLQ (Dead Letter Queue) so as not to lose money on critical paths. Attendees will get a verifiable set of rules for designing high-load systems and metrics that allow finding real bottlenecks in the infrastructure.
