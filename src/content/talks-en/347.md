More than 10 thousand experiments run at T-Bank every year, and many of them overlap. The question arises: can the results of such experiments be trusted, or does the overlap distort the effect (interference) and a retest is required? The question is especially acute, for example, in tests of marketing communication campaigns, where various pushes and SMS compete for the user's limited attention.

The topic has been hyped for a long time, and an experiment isolation system is an essential foundation of mature A/B platforms. Yet no one can articulate how to estimate the scale of the influence. Because of this, interference remains a semi-mythical problem: some ignore it entirely, others isolate part of the experiments based on expert judgment, and still others prefer to isolate almost everything, sacrificing the speed of running experiments.

At the same time, we built a complete mathematical model, derived an original criterion for assessing the influence of one experiment on another and developed an internal tool for on-the-fly checking.

Attendees will be introduced to the interference model, learn how to estimate it and what the size of the distortion depends on. We will share research results from T-Bank: we will answer the question of interference scale, show interesting cases and also share internal insights.
