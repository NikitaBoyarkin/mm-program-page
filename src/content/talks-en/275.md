E-commerce platforms constantly experiment with internal promotion algorithms and pricing. However, when testing minimum ad rate thresholds, a classic A/B test turns out to be legally impossible: randomly splitting sellers into test and control groups means giving them different commercial terms, which directly violates antitrust law. This creates a deadlock: how do you measure price elasticity and test product hypotheses when classic user randomization is strictly prohibited? The talk covers RWB's experience of estimating causal relationships under hard constraints using the synthetic control method.

The speaker will show the internal mechanics of the tool: changes are rolled out not to individual sellers but to entire product categories. You will learn how regression models based on "donor categories" with similar historical behavior are used to predict a synthetic control trend for the test group. Special attention will be paid to testing statistical significance in complex non-parametric approaches — Kuramagomed will walk through the math of calculating it via a permutation test to estimate the real incremental effect of a price change.

Listeners will get a detailed "in plain terms" explanation of how the synthetic control method works and will understand its mathematical connection to the popular Difference-in-Differences approach. The talk will give product analysts and data scientists a ready-made framework for legally and statistically valid testing of pricing hypotheses where standard A/B platforms are completely powerless.
