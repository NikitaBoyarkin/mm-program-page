Data-driven decisions turn out to be wrong far more often than companies are willing to admit. The main reason lies not in processing tools but in a lack of statistical intuition: algorithms generate numbers, and people draw wrong conclusions from them. The talk examines the problem of blind trust in reports and algorithms through the prism of five fundamental errors of analysis.

The speaker, who developed the statistical engine of the VWO platform and taught data analytics to the editorial team of The Economist, will use live examples to break down source distortion and the null hypothesis trap, when absence of evidence is taken as evidence of absence. Special attention is paid to how the cost of false positives and false negatives should directly drive product decisions. You will learn which critical anomalies are hidden behind averaged metrics, and why the chance of randomly getting a winner in an A/B test is mathematically not equal to the probability that the winner is false.

Listeners will get a set of frameworks for checking any analytics (machine or manual). The talk will help data analysts, product managers and marketers develop an intuitive understanding of statistics without diving deep into formulas and will teach them to ask the right questions that break down wrong conclusions in dashboards.
