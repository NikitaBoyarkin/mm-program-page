Handing the classification of texts (sentiment, topics, categories) over to language models made labeling fast and cheap. However, along with this the status of the numbers the business sees on dashboards changed: the target metric is no longer observed directly but predicted. The industry tends to assume that checking the classifier's overall accuracy is enough to launch a pipeline, but in practice this approach leads to fundamental distortions in the estimation of product and marketing effects.

The talk examines the mathematical nature of the error in neural-network labeling: the model does not err randomly but where the text is harder. The bias in a share depends on the share itself, so data from different segments is not comparable, and if the difficulty of texts changes over time, a dynamics that does not exist appears in the metrics. The speaker will show why in A/B tests classic randomization does not save you from a distorted measurement of the outcome. You will learn how an LLM classifier can compress a real effect, depriving the experiment of its built-in statistical power and invalidating the MDE calculation. Separately, the popular myth that a consensus of several different LLMs guarantees reliability will be dismantled (spoiler: their errors are often correlated).

Attendees will get a rigorous statistical method for correcting these distortions based on prediction-powered inference. The talk will provide a ready algorithm: how, using a small manually labeled validation sample, to estimate and subtract the model's bias. You will learn how to build a pipeline in which the unbiasedness of the final estimates is guaranteed by the sampling design rather than by the quality of the neural network itself.
