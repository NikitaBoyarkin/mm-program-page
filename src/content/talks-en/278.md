Traditional qualitative analysis of customer communications does not scale: manual call listening covers only a small share of dialogues. As a result, a mass of unstructured information (objections, insights, behavior patterns) remains a blind spot for analytics. The talk examines a method for transforming raw voice logs into structured datasets using neural networks. On a dataset from the UIS platform, the speaker will show how to build automatic transcription, intent tagging and emotion recognition in order to turn speech into metrics suitable for quantitative analysis.

The talk is based on a detailed breakdown of three applied business cases. First: parsing a mass of calls to identify real objections, which made it possible to rebuild product pages and double the conversion to inquiries on current traffic. Second: building a process for predictively identifying dissatisfied customers, which reduced the number of negative reviews by 70%. Third: a 6% increase in success conversion through adjusting scripts based on semantic analysis. The speaker will justify the methods used and examine the operational limitations of AI models: transcription cost, accuracy problems in specific niches, and the team's own mistakes during implementation.

Attendees will learn how to build a pipeline for turning unstructured speech into tabular data for research. The talk will provide an understanding of the methodology: how to correctly tag calls and link speech analytics data to business results. You will understand which tasks machine dialogue analysis is economically justified in, and which — where it fails and requires manual validation or classic research methods.
