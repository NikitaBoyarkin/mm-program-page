Building analytics under extreme loads often leads to incidents. The talk examines a production case from Sber: the system processed a stream of 200 thousand events per second with an accumulated base of half a trillion records. An attempt to compute URL state aggregations "on the fly" via a Materialized View in ClickHouse led to Too many parts, a halt of backfill processes and the formation of a lag of a billion events in the main realtime loop.

The speaker will break down this architectural mistake in detail and show how the incident forced the team to completely rebuild the system. You will learn why the business requirement to "see the metric quickly" does not mean realtime computation. The audience will study a batch loop of four layers (Raw → State → Mart → History). We will break down how to compute the historical state of an object from a compact State layer instead of reading the entire base of raw events, and how to publish a mart atomically so as to free dashboards from the "flickering" of incomplete data.

As a result you will get a proven way of building high-load pipelines for preparing ML datasets, crawling and ETL. The talk will give clear criteria: when complex analytics must be moved into a separate batch loop, how to embed business logic in the data model, and why abandoning streaming aggregation of global states makes the system resilient.
