Even after corporate AI is officially rolled out and training is conducted, data leaks continue. Facing tight deadlines, employees tend to optimize their work at the expense of security: they copy a manager's assignment in full, along with closed financial terms, and give third-party AI services excessive access to the whole work disk instead of selecting specific files. A copy of confidential information is created on the external provider's side at the moment of transfer, that is, long before any actual cyberattack. The talk is about moving from a formal ban to an architecture that limits what AI assistants can do at the hardware and process level.

The talk examines technical leak mechanisms in detail using two scenarios: access to stored copies (exposure of database logs, on the DeepSeek example) and data extraction inside an active session (the RovoBlast vulnerability). The speakers will show how to build a perimeter in which an employee's right to read a document does not mean the AI application's right to receive and process it. The process of configuring the target logic will be covered: token control, management of the actual access scope (restriction to specific files) and monitoring of request logs. Separately, the course of action for the security team when data transfer has already happened is considered.

Attendees will learn how to design a "green corridor" for securely performing typical tasks and how to record the rules in an AI solution passport (owner, data sources, available actions). The talk will give a methodology for secure prompting: how to split a request into generating the document structure and the subsequent addition of sensitive numbers in a protected environment. You will get a framework for testing employee skills through simulation of work processes with dummy data, which makes it possible to assess how secure the work really is, and not just whether blocks fired.
