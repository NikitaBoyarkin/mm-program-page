Estimating the economic effect of business expansion under limited resources is a complex task, since classic A/B testing does not work here. In the Yandex.Drive case, opening new service zones in a city with a shared fleet inevitably led to cannibalization of old zones. Running a per-user test is impossible because of network effects, and using switchback experiments (by geo and time) leads to the opened geography starting to unpredictably “flicker.” To bypass these limitations, the team developed an approach to experimentation using a synthetic control group. A carsharing simulator — a system dynamics simulation model — is used as the synthetic control group, acting as the control group.

The talk covers the simulator's architecture and the process of calibrating it to actual business metrics. The model describes the equilibrium between demand, car availability, conversion, orders, and fleet utilization. You will learn how, with such a simulator, to forecast changes in key indicators: total revenue, dynamic profit, car idle time, and revenue per car-day when varying business parameters (geography, average check, order duration). The speaker will justify the use of simulation modeling in conditions where other incrementality evaluation methods are inapplicable, and will highlight situations in which this approach does not work.

Listeners will learn how to apply system dynamics to estimate revenue increment and sales cannibalization. The talk will show the process of creating a test environment that allows forecasting the behavior of complex systems under changes in external conditions (for example, how the first snow will affect demand and the SLA of the delivery service). As a result, you will get a working tool for tuning dynamic pricing algorithms and dealing with excess supply in services with shared resources.
