When it comes to evaluating marketing effectiveness, analysts are used to relying on high-frequency digital channel data. However, the harsh reality of large brands is a complex mix of digital and offline, often launched in “flights” (bursts) with weekly aggregation. As a result, after three years of active campaigns you have only about 150 rows of data. Under such conditions, classic ML (linear regression, XGBoost) breaks down and produces negative weights for channels that actually work, while time-series models (Prophet) err in forecasting during a flight-based advertising campaign and do not answer the main business question — “what drove the sales?”

The talk breaks down in detail the process of building marketing mix models (MMM) based on Bayesian regression — an approach that lets you extract the maximum of insights from a minimal amount of information. The speaker will justify why this particular mathematical apparatus saves you in conditions of data hunger and will show how to integrate business rules directly into the model through prior distributions (priors). In practice, you will work through how to correctly program adstock (the effect of fading memory of advertising) and saturation (diminishing returns on investment).

As a result, you will take away a step-by-step guide to assembling a Bayesian MMM for “flight” placements. The talk will arm you with an exhaustive model diagnostics checklist: from purely statistical metrics (like R-hat) to calibration on external data and sanity checks. You will learn to build offline advertising evaluation models in a way that the business will finally believe.
